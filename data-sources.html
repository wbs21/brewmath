Sources, formulas and limits

Numbers should have a trail.

BrewMath uses public brewing models, official style ranges and traceable ingredient references. This page explains the basis of those numbers and where an estimate should not be treated as a measurement.

Data catalog version 2026.11.24 · Updated 24 November 2026

Important:

IBU, ABV, colour, carbonation and water calculations are estimates. Ingredient age, process, equipment and measurement error can all matter more than small formula differences.

BJCP style ranges

BrewMath extracts only the official style codes, names and numerical ranges. Flavour descriptions are not copied from the guidelines. Style context is shown for comparison, not as a score or certification.

IBU models

BrewMath implements both Tinseth and Rager. Hop-form corrections include pellet, whole-leaf and hop-plug assumptions. Dry hopping contributes 0 IBU in the bitterness model because aroma, flavour and polyphenol extraction are outside IBU.

Whirlpool bitterness uses an explicitly approximate half-time equivalent. There is no universal model that captures every commercial kettle, whirlpool and cooling system.

Gravity, Brix and colour

  • Specific gravity and °Plato conversion uses a common cubic approximation of ASBC reference values.
  • Brix in BrewMath is a sucrose-equivalent conversion. It is not corrected for alcohol in a fermented sample.
  • Colour uses the Morey model and reports SRM with an approximate EBC conversion.
  • Recipe colour is an estimate and may differ from a measured laboratory result.

Carbonation

Priming calculations account for residual CO₂ using the highest fermentation temperature, then estimate sugar against the volume actually packaged. Carbonation drops and sugar-source differences can change the result.

Safety first: Packaging above the safe pressure range for a bottle creates a real risk of injury. Follow the bottle manufacturer's pressure rating and do not rely on a calculator to make an unsafe package safe.

Water chemistry

Salt additions use chemical formulas and molar masses, including water of crystallisation. Residual alkalinity uses the Kolbach approximation. Mash strike temperature uses a heat-balance equation.

Your water report, grain temperature, equipment thermal mass and mineral assumptions can change the result. Calibrate against your own system rather than treating the first estimate as a fixed equipment profile.

Ingredient data

Ingredient records use manufacturer or breeding-program information where available. PPG, Lovibond, alpha acid, attenuation and temperature values are typical values, not guarantees for the package in front of you.

Common-reference entries are marked as unverified secondary data rather than being presented as manufacturer specifications. Manufacturer links may point to a current catalogue rather than one immutable product document.

Versioned data and validation

BrewMath's external reference data is stored as versioned JSON datasets with record IDs, dataset versions, source metadata and retrieval dates. Automated validation checks duplicate IDs, invalid ranges, non-finite values, source references and required metadata.

This makes the data inspectable and allows corrections to ship with an app update without reintroducing scattered hard-coded tables.

What BrewMath does not claim

  • It does not claim laboratory accuracy.
  • It does not claim BJCP certification or endorsement.
  • It does not treat a flavour match as an official style classification.
  • It does not infer certainty when published models disagree.
  • It does not replace sensory judgement or measured process data.
